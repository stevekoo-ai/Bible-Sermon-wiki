# 🎙️ Audrey: 2 Corinthians, the Forest and the Trees

> 한국어판: [[고린도후서_숲과나무]]

## 🌲 The Forest

Okay, let's zoom out first.

2 Corinthians is Paul's most personal letter. He wrote it after things got really messy with this church. The main idea is pretty simple. God's power shows up best in our weakness.

To give you a bit of context, here's what happened between 1 and 2 Corinthians.

- Paul sent 1 Corinthians. It didn't fully land.
- So he made a quick visit. It went badly. Someone in the church openly insulted him.
- He went home and wrote a letter "with many tears." We don't have that one anymore.
- Then things got really rough in Asia. He says they "despaired of life itself."
- He waited for Titus to bring news. Titus didn't show up. Paul couldn't rest.
- Finally, Titus showed up with good news. The church had turned around.
- That's when Paul wrote 2 Corinthians. He was in Macedonia, around AD 55 or 56.

Now the structure. Think of it as three chunks.

1. **Chapters 1 to 7.** Relief and reconciliation. Basically, "Here's what my ministry really looks like."
2. **Chapters 8 and 9.** A fundraising ask. It's an offering for poor believers in Jerusalem.
3. **Chapters 10 to 13.** The tone changes completely. Paul pushes back on the so-called "super-apostles." He gets sarcastic. And he brags about his weakness.

Quick side note. Some scholars think chapters 10 to 13 were a separate letter. But every manuscript we have keeps it as one letter.

Here's the one-line difference. 1 Corinthians is about the church's problems. 2 Corinthians is about the minister's heart.

## 🌳 The Trees

| Ch. | In one line | Key verse (ESV) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | God comforts us, so we can comfort others. | "...so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction." (1:4) |
| 2 | Forgive the guy who hurt you. | "...you should rather turn to forgive and comfort him." (2:7) |
| 3 | You are our letter of recommendation. | "...you are a letter from Christ." (3:3) |
| 4 | Treasure in clay jars. | "But we have this treasure in jars of clay." (4:7) |
| 5 | A new creation, with a new job. | "...if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." (5:17) |
| 6 | Now is the time. Sad, but still joyful. | "...as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing." (6:10) |
| 7 | The good kind of sorrow. | "For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation..." (7:10) |
| 8 | Jesus became poor for you. | "...though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor." (8:9) |
| 9 | Give with a happy heart. | "...God loves a cheerful giver." (9:7) |
| 10 | Take every thought captive. | "...take every thought captive to obey Christ." (10:5) |
| 11 | Paul's "fool's speech," plus everything he suffered. | "If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness." (11:30) |
| 12 | The thorn in the flesh. | "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness." (12:9) |
| 13 | Check yourselves. Then a blessing. | "Examine yourselves, to see whether you are in the faith." (13:5) |

## 💬 Audrey's take, just for Steve

Steve, you've been feeling pretty small at work lately. I get it.

Why don't we read chapter 12 together this week? Paul asked God three times to take the thorn away. God said no. And that "no" turned out to be grace.

Being the junior guy isn't the end of the story. Honestly, it might be the best spot to see God's power.
